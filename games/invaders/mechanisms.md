# Space Invaders — how the machine works

This map describes the parts of Space Invaders (Intel 8080, Midway `mw8080bw` board) whose behaviour has
been recovered into the idiomatic layer. It is a **current-state** description of the machine, not a history
of the port. Every claim is tagged with the confidence of its reading:

- **[seen]** — a role confirmed by a MAME observation.
- **[code]** — a role read from the routine bodies; MAME grounding still open. A first stage-B grounding pass
  ran against a MAME play capture (boot + attract + a coin/start/1-player game): the sound-port shadows, the
  living-alien count, the game-active gate, and the framebuffer draw/clear routines with their video-RAM
  boundary constants are **[seen]**. The other helpers described below — the player-shot and shield
  primitives, the fleet-march sound beat, the alien-shot rate selection, the object work-buffer and record
  staging, the per-player save/input helpers, the scoring accumulator's inputs, and the frame-task and boot
  setup — are read from the code and stay **[code]** pending a two-player / saucer / longer capture. The
  per-item tag lives in names.js (a cell's inline `// [seen]`/`// [code]`, a routine's `cert`).
- **[guess]** — an unclear reading; these keep a `loc_<addr>` placeholder rather than a descriptive name.

What is described here is the layer of **helper routines** — the primitives the game's spine calls to touch
video, sound, the alien field, the player shot and shields, the per-player records, scoring, and the frame
timers. The game's control-flow spine (the main loop, the object-state dispatcher and its handlers, the
interrupt handlers) is not yet recovered and is **not** described yet; a routine named `loc_<addr>` anywhere
below is one still awaiting a descriptive name — its mechanism may already be read at [code], its game-role or
naming still open.

## Video RAM and the framebuffer

The screen lives in a single contiguous block of memory. It begins at `VIDEO_RAM_BASE` = 0x2400 [seen] and runs up to `VIDEO_RAM_END` = 0x4000 [seen], so the framebuffer is 0x1c00 (7168) bytes. Every bit is a pixel: a byte holds eight stacked pixels, and the block is organized as a stack of 0x20-byte (32-byte) lines. That geometry falls straight out of the code — 7168 bytes divided by the 0x20 stride the drawing loops use is exactly 0xe0 (224) lines, each 0x20 × 8 = 256 pixels wide [code]. Within a line, stepping the address by +1 moves eight pixels along; stepping by +0x20 drops to the same position on the next line down, which is why every routine that walks the display vertically adds 0x20 per pass.

Wiping the display comes in two flavors. `clearScreen` [seen] zeroes the whole framebuffer indiscriminately: it starts at `VIDEO_RAM_BASE` and stores 0x00 into each byte, bumping the pointer and stopping the moment the high byte reaches 0x40 — that is, when the pointer hits `VIDEO_RAM_END` [seen]. `clearPlayfield` [seen] is more surgical. It starts two bytes in, at `PLAYFIELD_VRAM_BASE` = 0x2402 [seen], and only clears the interior of each line: it writes 0, advances, and whenever the low five bits of the address reach 0x1c it jumps the pointer forward by six, skipping a six-byte margin at the end of every 32-byte line before resuming on the next one; it loops until the high byte reaches 0x40 [seen]. The effect is that each line contributes a run of interior bytes to the play area while a six-byte gutter at its edge is left untouched — the score/credit trim around the active field.

Placing a sprite at an arbitrary pixel requires turning a coordinate into a byte address plus a sub-byte bit offset, and `coordToScreenAddr` [seen] does the address half. It takes the 16-bit value in HL, shifts it right by three (an integer divide-by-eight, so the low three bits — the pixel's position inside its byte — fall away), and then forces the high byte into the framebuffer window by masking it to 0x3f and OR-ing in 0x20. The result is guaranteed to land in the 0x2000–0x3fff page that contains video RAM. `seatBlitPosition` [code] handles the other half and pairs with it: before folding the coordinate, it takes the low three bits of L — precisely the intra-byte offset that the divide-by-eight discarded — and writes them to output port 2, then falls through into `coordToScreenAddr`. So the two together seat a blit at any pixel: the byte address in HL and the fractional bit offset latched to the hardware at port 2 [code].

Filling and drawing all share the +0x20 vertical walk. `fillScreenRow` [seen] is the workhorse: over B passes it stores the same byte (A) at the pointer and advances the pointer by 0x20 each time, leaving HL one stride past the end when it finishes — a solid run down the framebuffer. Two thin wrappers seat its arguments. `clearScreenStrip` [code] zeroes A first and then runs the fill, blanking a run of B lines with 0x00. `drawBottomLine` [code] seats a lit byte of 0x01, a count of 0xe0 (224) lines, and a start of `PLAYFIELD_VRAM_BASE`, then runs the same fill — painting one lit byte at the play-field's edge offset all the way down every one of the 224 lines, which is the full-length boundary line at the base of the field (the 0xe0 count is itself the confirmation that the framebuffer is 224 lines tall).

Sprites are copied in rather than filled. `drawSpriteColumn` [seen] reads B source bytes from a stream (DE) and lays them down a single column: each byte is written to the destination, the source advances by one, and the destination advances by 0x20 to the next line. A count of zero wraps to a full 256-byte pass. On top of it sit two callers. `drawSprite8x8` [code] resolves a sprite id in A to its source bytes at loc_1e00 + 8·A — loc_1e00 [guess] being the base of the 8-byte-per-entry sprite bitmap table — writes A to output port 6, and blits eight rows down through `drawSpriteColumn`, leaving HL live. `drawSpriteColumn16` [code] is the same draw with the row count fixed to 0x10 (16), and it takes care to preserve BC around the call so its caller's loop counters survive.

Two routines handle rectangular regions rather than single columns, and they are mirror images used to save and restore what a sprite is about to overwrite. `captureScreenRect` [code] reads a rectangle out of the screen: for each of B rows it reads C bytes starting from a row base in HL, streaming them into a contiguous destination at DE, then advances the row base by 0x20 to the next line; it returns the advanced destination end and the next source row base. `orBlitBitmap` [seen] goes the other way and merges bitmap data in without erasing what is already there: for each of B rows it OR-combines C source bytes from DE into the destination at HL, then advances the destination by 0x20 to the next line while the source stream runs straight on. `drawOrSaveShields` [code] ties the pair together for the four player shields: it stashes a mode byte to `SHIELD_SAVE_RESTORE_MODE` = 0x2081 [code], then walks four blocks of 0x16 (22) rows by 0x02 (2) columns starting at `SHIELD_VRAM_BASE` = 0x2806 [code]; when the mode flag is set it captures each block off-screen, and when it is clear it OR-blits the stored bitmap back in. Between blocks it steps the screen pointer by `DRAW_BLOCK_STRIDE` = 0x02e0 [code], which is 23 lines — one more than each 22-line block, leaving a one-line gap between the stacked shields.

Underneath all of these sits `blockCopy` [seen], a plain byte-for-byte copy: it moves B bytes from DE to HL with both pointers advancing together (a count of zero meaning a full 256), and unlike the drawing loops it uses no 0x20 stride at all. It is the general staging primitive — used to prime work RAM, object records, sprite/animation templates, draw-sequence blocks, and the shield buffers — moving bitmaps and records into place so that the strided writers above can paint them onto the screen.

## Sound

All sound on this machine is driven by two write-only output ports, port 3 and port 5, each fronted by a one-byte shadow in RAM. Because the ports cannot be read back, the game keeps a mirror of what it last wrote so it can flip an individual sound on or off without disturbing the others: `SOUND_PORT3_SHADOW` at 0x2094 [seen] mirrors port 3, and `SOUND_PORT5_SHADOW` at 0x2098 [seen] mirrors port 5. Every sound operation is fundamentally the same move — edit a bit in the shadow, then push the whole shadow byte out the port.

### Port 3: the one-shot effect latch

Port 3 carries the discrete, momentary effects, and it is manipulated bit by bit through a matched pair of helpers. `startSound` at 0x18fa [seen] turns an effect *on*: it ORs the bits in register B into the 0x2094 shadow, stores the result back, mirrors it out port 3, and returns the new byte in A [seen]. Its mirror image, still carrying the name `loc_19dc` at 0x19dc [seen], turns effects *off*: it ANDs the shadow with B — a mask with the doomed bit(s) cleared — stores it back, mirrors out port 3, and likewise returns the byte in A [seen]. Between them these two routines let any part of the game raise or drop a single sound line while every other active effect keeps playing.

Each bit of the port-3 shadow is thus an independent effect channel, and callers around the game reach for these two helpers to toggle their own bit. The player-shot cue lives at bit 1: `loc_172c` at 0x172c [code] raises it with `startSound(0x02)` while a shot is live and clears it with the 0xfd mask otherwise. `loc_18f1` at 0x18f1 [code] composes B as either 0x02 or 0x03 depending on whether `ALIEN_COUNT` at 0x2082 [seen] has fallen to exactly one, feeding that into the same latch. Bit 3 is a scoring/prize cue, raised by `loc_0a5f` at 0x0a5f [code] with `startSound(0x08)` and cleared by `deactivatePrize` [code] with the 0xf7 mask when the prize is retired. The saucer's channel is bit 0: `stopSaucerSound` at 0x0707 [code] is a thin wrapper that loads B = 0xfe and drops straight into `loc_19dc`, clearing that one bit while leaving the rest of the effect latch intact. Other callers around the machine claim the remaining bits the same way — 0x04, 0x10, and 0x20 all appear as `startSound` masks — so the byte behaves as a small bank of on/off effect switches.

### Port 5: the fleet-march channel and its silencer

Port 5 is where the marching-aliens heartbeat plays, and its shadow is organized differently. The low nibble (mask 0x0f) holds the current march note as a one-hot value, and the two high bits (mask 0x30) hold persistent sounds that should survive between beats — for example `loc_074b` [code] ORs bit 4 (0x10) into the 0x2098 shadow before latching, seating a sound in that persistent region. Latching to port 5 goes through a small shared helper: `loc_176d` at 0x176d [code] reads the 0x2098 shadow, masks it to 0x30, and writes that out port 5 — silencing the low-nibble march note while leaving the two high, persistent bits sounding. A second entry point into the same tail, `latchSoundPort5` at 0x1770 [code], skips the shadow read and instead masks the *caller's* A to 0x30 before writing it out port 5; `loc_074b` uses this form right after it sets its bit, latching the freshly edited value.

### The fleet-march engine

The signature marching cadence is produced by two cooperating routines that run every frame, one keeping the beat and the other choosing the note and the tempo.

`stepFleetMarchSound` at 0x1740 [code] is the beat timer. It runs from the per-frame state handler `loc_0010` [code], which invokes it only while the game-in-progress flag at 0x20ef is set. Each call first decrements `FLEET_SOUND_OFF_TIMER` at 0x209b [code]; when that counter reaches zero it calls `loc_176d` to silence the note, which is what ends the short "on" window of each beat and gives the march its staccato feel. Next it checks the enable gate at loc_2068 (0x2068) [guess] — role open — and if that byte is clear it silences through `loc_176d` and bails, so the heartbeat is suppressed whenever the gate is down. With the gate up it decrements the main beat counter `FLEET_SOUND_TIMER` at 0x2096 [code]; until that counter expires it simply returns, letting the interval between beats elapse. When the beat counter *does* hit zero, it sounds the note by writing the 0x2098 shadow out port 5, and — provided `ALIEN_COUNT` at 0x2082 [seen] is still nonzero — re-arms for the next beat: it reloads `FLEET_SOUND_TIMER` from `FLEET_SOUND_PERIOD` at 0x2097 [code], raises the step flag `FLEET_SOUND_STEP` at 0x2095 [code] to 1, and reloads `FLEET_SOUND_OFF_TIMER` to 4 (the length of the on-window before the next silence). If the last alien is gone, it silences and stops instead.

`advanceFleetMarchSound` at 0x1775 [code] is the note-and-tempo selector, run once per frame from the frame-loop tail `loc_0849` [code]. It acts only when the step flag at 0x2095 has been raised — that is, only on a frame where a beat just fired. On such a frame it reads the live `ALIEN_COUNT` (0x2082) [seen] and walks the threshold table `FLEET_RATE_THRESHOLDS` at 0x1a11 [code], advancing in lockstep through the parallel value table `FLEET_RATE_TABLE` at 0x1a21 [code] until the alien count is no longer below the current threshold; the matching table byte is stored into `FLEET_SOUND_PERIOD` (0x2097) [code]. Because that period is what the beat timer reloads, and because fewer surviving aliens select a shorter period, this is exactly the mechanism behind the famous speed-up: the fleet marches faster as it thins. Having set the tempo, it advances the note itself by rotating the low nibble of the 0x2098 shadow left by one — cycling 0x01, 0x02, 0x04, 0x08 and wrapping 0x10 back to 0x01 — while preserving the two high persistent bits (0x30), so the march steps through its four descending tones. It then clears the step flag.

Whether or not that beat block ran, `advanceFleetMarchSound` finishes by ticking a separate one-shot timer, `SFX_OFF_TIMER` at 0x2099 [code]. While that timer is still counting it returns with A = 0; only when it reaches zero does the routine reach over to the port-3 side and call `loc_19dc` with mask 0xef, clearing bit 4 of the port-3 effect latch. This is an automatic turn-off for a timed effect that was started on port 3 elsewhere: the effect is switched on and given a lifetime in `SFX_OFF_TIMER`, and the fleet-march pass silences it when its time is up. Note the two "off" timers are distinct — `FLEET_SOUND_OFF_TIMER` (0x209b) bounds the on-window of each march beat on port 5, while `SFX_OFF_TIMER` (0x2099) bounds a one-shot effect on port 3.

## The alien field and its march

Each of the two players keeps a private 256-byte page of state, and the machine tracks which one is currently on screen with a single high-byte selector, `ACTIVE_PLAYER_PAGE` at 0x2067 [code]. That byte holds only the page number — 0x21 for player one, 0x22 for player two — and every routine that needs the live player's data forms an address by shifting it left eight bits; `activePlayerPageBase` (0x1611) [code] does exactly this to produce the base of the active page. The bottom of each page is the alien roster: a flat table of 0x37 (55) one-byte cells, one per alien, laid out as eleven columns across five rows. Player one's table lives at `ALIEN_FIELD_P1` = 0x2100 [code] and player two's at `ALIEN_FIELD_P2` = 0x2200 [code]. A cell reads 0x01 while its alien is alive and 0x00 once it has been shot; the table is nothing more than a bitmap of who is still on the board.

A round begins by declaring every alien alive. `markAllAliensAlive` (0x01c3) [seen] is the primitive that does it — it simply writes 0x01 into 0x37 consecutive bytes starting from a base address handed to it. Two thin wrappers pin that base to a specific player: `markAllAliensAliveP1` (0x01c0) [code] seats the base at 0x2100 and falls straight into the fill, and `markAllAliensAliveP2` (0x1904) [code] seats it at 0x2200 and does the same. Game and round setup (the initialization pass at loc_079b [guess], the player-handoff at loc_09ef [guess], and the per-round setup at loc_0aea [guess]) reset one or both rosters this way, so both players start each life with a full, undamaged formation.

The fleet does not store 55 separate positions. Instead the game tracks one *reference alien* — the anchor of the whole grid — and derives every other alien's screen position from it. The reference coordinate is a two-byte pair held in the working cells at loc_2009 and loc_200a [guess], whose exact axis roles are not yet pinned; together they name the origin from which the formation is measured. `loadReferenceAlienState` (0x00b1) [code] restores this anchor when a player resumes. It builds a pointer into the top of the active player's page — `activeFieldRecordPointer` (0x0886) [code] forms `(mem[0x2067] << 8) | 0xfc`, i.e. the record slot near the very top of the page — reads the saved 16-bit coordinate word stored there, and mirrors that word into two places at once: the live reference pair at loc_2009 [guess] and the draw cursor `ALIEN_DRAW_ADDR` at 0x200b [code]. It then reads the byte one below that record slot (page:0xfb), treats it as the fleet's current step/delta, drops it by one only when it reads exactly 0x03, and stores the result into loc_2008 [guess]. From that same byte it derives the march direction: `FLEET_MOVE_DIR` at 0x200d [code] is set to 0x01 when the saved step is the 0xfe sentinel and cleared otherwise, so a player picks up marching in the same direction they left off. (The inverse save, staging the working state back into the page record, is performed elsewhere by `stageActivePlayerFieldSave` (0x0878) [code], reading loc_2008 and the loc_2009 word.)

Turning an alien's roster index into pixels is the job of `alienIndexToScreenCoords` (0x017a) [code]. Given an index in register L, it seeds B and C from the reference pair at loc_2009 and loc_200a [guess], then divides the index by 0x0b (eleven, the column count): each whole subtraction of eleven steps B by 0x10 and bumps a row counter in D, and the leftover remainder then steps C by 0x10 that many times. In other words the formation is a regular grid — columns spaced 0x10 apart along one axis, rows spaced 0x10 apart along the other, all offset from the single reference coordinate — and the routine returns the resolved position in L (the row-stepped coordinate) and C (the column-stepped coordinate) together with the row number 0..4 in D, which downstream code uses to pick the alien's sprite type. The same coordinate resolver is also reached from loc_0563 [guess], which needs an alien's position for its own purposes.

Only one alien is redrawn per frame, and two cooperating routines hand the work between them through `ALIEN_DRAW_PENDING` at 0x2000 [code] — a flag with exactly two writers. The advance pass at loc_0141 [guess] returns immediately if 0x2000 is already set (a draw is still outstanding), otherwise it walks the roster table at `(0x2067:index)` starting just past a rolling cursor at 0x2006, looking for the next cell that is still alive (its value decrements to zero), wrapping around at 0x37. It records that index back at 0x2006, calls `alienIndexToScreenCoords` to resolve the alien's position, folds the result into the draw cursor `ALIEN_DRAW_ADDR` (0x200b) [code], and finally sets `ALIEN_DRAW_PENDING` = 0x01 to signal that a specific alien is now staged. The blit pass at loc_0100 [guess] then consumes that: it reads the object's frame byte, builds a sprite pointer from base 0x1c00, and — when the object's animation flag is set — calls `selectAlternateSpriteFrame` (0x013b) [code], which advances the sprite pointer by 0x30 into the second sprite bank. That 0x30 bump is the whole of the marching animation: it swaps between the alien's two leg poses. The blit pass loads `ALIEN_DRAW_ADDR` (0x200b) [code] as its destination, draws the sprite, and clears `ALIEN_DRAW_PENDING` (0x2000) [code] back to zero, releasing the flag for the next frame's advance.

The population is taken fresh by `countLiveAliens` (0x15f3) [code]. It points at the active player's roster via `activePlayerPageBase` (0x1611) [code], scans all 0x37 cells counting the non-zero (alive) ones, and publishes the tally into `ALIEN_COUNT` at 0x2082 [seen]. When exactly one alien remains it also raises `LAST_ALIEN_FLAG` at 0x206b [code] (that lone-survivor flag is written here; no reader for it appears among these routines, so its consumer is open). `ALIEN_COUNT` is the pivot that gives Space Invaders its signature acceleration: the main frame loop at loc_081f [guess] runs the count and, when 0x2082 falls to zero — the whole formation cleared — hands off to loc_09ef [guess] to switch player or advance the round; the fleet-sound tempo code (`stepFleetMarchSound` at 0x1740 [code] and `advanceFleetMarchSound` at 0x1775 [code]) indexes a rate table by the live count so the march quickens as aliens die; loc_18f1 [code] uses it to choose the fleet's downward step size (2, or 3 when a single alien is left); and loc_08d8 [code] trips a separate state cell once fewer than nine aliens survive. Fewer aliens, faster and lower march.

The horizontal march and its edge-reversal turn on `FLEET_MOVE_DIR` (0x200d) [code]. The step routine at loc_1597 [guess] reads that flag to decide which boundary reference to test, and when the fleet reaches an edge it commits a new direction back into 0x200d together with the movement delta in loc_2008 [guess] — the sideways step is the 0xfe sentinel (a two-pixel move in one direction), and a turn substitutes the downward step size chosen by loc_18f1 [code]. That 0xfe sentinel is precisely the value `loadReferenceAlienState` looks for when it restores 0x200d, closing the loop between a saved fleet and a resumed one. The reference coordinate has reach beyond the fleet itself: the saucer timer at loc_0913 [code] gates on loc_2009 [guess] being below 0x78, so the flying saucer can only appear once the formation has marched far enough down the screen.

## Player shot, shields, and object strips

Every moving thing this part of the machine draws — a shot, a sprite, a shield — is described the same way: a compact record in work RAM that the code reads, folds into a video-RAM address, and blits. The routines here are the plumbing that reads those records, arms and paces the player's shot, and paints or preserves the four shields.

### The 5-byte object descriptor

The common shape is a five-byte descriptor read by `loadSpriteDescriptor` (0x1a3b) [code]. It walks the pointer in `HL` and pulls the bytes in order into `DE`, then `A`, `C`, `B`: the first two bytes become a 16-bit value in `DE` (a graphics/source pointer), the next two are recombined so that `HL := (C << 8) | A` — the coordinate word held in the descriptor's third and fourth bytes — and the fifth byte lands in `B` as a size/row count. So one descriptor carries a source-image pointer, a position, and a height, and after the read `DE` points at the artwork while `HL` holds the position.

A raw coordinate is not yet a screen address. `coordToScreenAddr` (0x1a47) [seen] does that fold: it shifts the coordinate right by three (eight display bits pack into one byte) and forces the high byte into the 0x2000–0x3fff video-RAM page (`((x >> 3) >> 8 & 0x3f) | 0x20`). `resolveSpriteScreenAddr` (0x0742) [code] chains the two together for a sprite record kept at `loc_2087` [guess]: it reads that descriptor with `loadSpriteDescriptor`, then folds the resulting coordinate through `coordToScreenAddr`, leaving `HL` = the on-screen destination and `DE` = the graphics pointer — exactly the pair a blit wants.

The player's shot is loaded a step shorter. `loadPlayerShotDescriptor` (0x0430) [code] points at the shot record's descriptor at `PLAYER_SHOT_DESC` (0x2027) [code] and runs the same five-byte read, but it does **not** fold the coordinate — it leaves `HL` holding the descriptor's `C:A` word directly. Its callers (the object handler at loc_03bb [guess]) hand that word straight to the sprite-draw helpers without a coordinate conversion, so for the shot record the third/fourth bytes are already a usable video-RAM address rather than a coordinate needing translation. `PLAYER_SHOT_DESC` sits inside the shot's object block that begins at `PLAYER_SHOT_STATUS` (0x2025) [code]; that whole block is reset from a seven-byte ROM template when a shot is (re)initialized.

### Arming and pacing the player shot

`PLAYER_SHOT_STATUS` (0x2025) [code] is the shot's one-at-a-time gate and its sound trigger. It is armed by `advanceRoundState` (0x1618) [code], which only acts once the round is armed and the field is idle, and which refuses outright if `PLAYER_SHOT_STATUS` is already non-zero — a live shot blocks a new one. During play (with `GAME_IN_PROGRESS` set) the fire button is debounced through `FIRE_BUTTON_LATCH` (0x202d) [code]: if the latch is set, the code only waits for the player to *release* the button (reading input bit 0x10) and clears the latch when they do; a new shot is armed only on a fresh press against a clear latch, at which point it stamps `PLAYER_SHOT_STATUS = 1` and re-sets `FIRE_BUTTON_LATCH = 1`. This is what stops a held button from auto-firing. In attract mode (`GAME_IN_PROGRESS` clear) the same gate instead stamps `PLAYER_SHOT_STATUS = 1` and advances the demo pointer, wrapping its low byte back to the start of the march window — the demo "fires" through the identical path.

The status cell is also read for audio: `loc_172c` (0x172c) [code] raises the shot-fire sound bit via `startSound(0x02)` when `PLAYER_SHOT_STATUS` is non-zero, and masks that bit back off (`loc_19dc(0xfd)` [seen]) when it is clear, so the firing tone tracks the shot's existence.

### Paging objects through the shared 11-byte strip

Shots are not stepped in place inside their own records. Instead each object is copied into one shared 11-byte scratch strip at `loc_2073` [guess] (role open; observed as the working-object buffer), mutated there, and copied back. `copyRecordToWorkBuffer` (0x0550) [code] first stashes the caller's `A` at `loc_207f` [guess], then block-copies eleven bytes (0x0b) from the source record in `DE` into `loc_2073` — priming the strip. Its twin `copyWorkBufferToRecord` (0x055b) [code] block-copies the same eleven bytes from `loc_2073` back out to the destination in `HL`, restoring the (now-stepped) strip into its record. Both ride the shared `blockCopy` (0x1a32) [seen].

Several shot-stepping routines (loc_0476, loc_04b6, loc_050f [guess]) use exactly this dance: prime the strip from an object record, step it, then page it back with the restore helper. The stepper itself, `loc_0563` [guess] (role open), works entirely against `loc_2073`: bit 0x80 of the first byte marks the object in-flight (set/clear as it lives and dies), bit 0x01 is its draw/pending request, a 16-bit pointer partway through the strip walks a data list, and the position accumulates as it advances. The value stashed at `loc_207f` by the prime step is read back by the stepper as a travel bound — which is why the caller's `A` is preserved across the copy.

### Choosing the shot cadence

How fast that stepper is allowed to advance is chosen by `selectAlienShotRate` (0x170e) [code]. It takes a key from the active player's object record — the second byte at `currentPlayerRecordPtr()+1` (0x09ca [code] selects player 1's or player 2's record) — a field-progression figure that changes as the game wears on. It then scans two parallel four-entry ROM tables in lockstep: `ALIEN_SHOT_RATE_THRESHOLDS` (0x1cb8) [code] for the first threshold that is greater-than-or-equal-to the key, and copies the matching byte from `ALIEN_SHOT_RATE_TABLE` (0x1aa1) [code] into `loc_20cf` [guess]. If the key exceeds all four thresholds the scan falls to the entry just past them, a default floor. `loc_20cf` is precisely the cadence cell the stepper `loc_0563` reads (comparing it against per-object counters) to decide whether a shot moves this tick — so `selectAlienShotRate` is the knob that speeds fire up as the round advances, and the stepper is where that choice takes effect.

### Shields: seed, save, restore

Each player owns four shields, held as bitmaps in a work-RAM buffer — player 1's at 0x2142, player 2's at 0x2242 — and drawn as a column of four blocks down the screen. `initShieldBuffers` (0x01f8) [code] seeds a fresh set: starting from the buffer base in `HL` it replicates a 0x2c-byte (44-byte) shield template from `loc_1d20` [guess] four times into consecutive slots, so the four buffers begin identical and intact, and returns `HL` one past the end.

`drawOrSaveShields` (0x021e) [code] moves those bitmaps between buffer and screen, and one flag decides the direction. It writes the caller's `A` to `SHIELD_SAVE_RESTORE_MODE` (0x2081) [code], then runs four passes over 22×2 blocks (`B=0x16` rows, `C=0x02` columns) starting at the screen base `SHIELD_VRAM_BASE` (0x2806) [code], stepping down by `DRAW_BLOCK_STRIDE` (0x02e0) [code] between shields. When the mode flag is **set**, each pass runs `captureScreenRect` (0x147c) [code], copying the on-screen shield region *into* the player's buffer — a save, taken when the active player yields so the incoming player's damaged shields are preserved. When the flag is **clear**, each pass runs `orBlitBitmap` (0x1a69) [seen], OR-merging the buffered bitmap *onto* the screen — a draw/restore. The four thin entry points wire the two arguments together: they preset `A` to 1 (save) or 0 (draw) and pick the player buffer (`DE = 0x2142` or `0x2242`) before entering the shared body, so saving player 1, saving player 2, restoring player 1, and restoring player 2 are all the same loop with the flag and buffer swapped.

## Per-player records and input

Space Invaders runs two players out of the same code by keeping each player's world on its own memory page and flipping a single selector byte between rounds. That selector is `ACTIVE_PLAYER_PAGE` at 0x2067 [code], and it does double duty: its whole value is the high byte of the active player's page, while its low bit records *which* player is up. The two players sit on adjacent pages whose numbers differ only in that low bit, so one byte answers both "where is this player's state?" and "is this player one or player two?" at once — and every routine here either shifts the byte up to reach a page, or masks its low bit to pick a per-player resource.

**Forming page addresses.** `activePlayerPageBase` at 0x1611 [code] is the plainest of these: it reads 0x2067 and shifts it left eight bits, handing back `HL = mem[0x2067] << 8`, i.e. the very bottom of the active player's page. `countLiveAliens` (at 0x15f3) leans on it directly to walk the alien grid: starting from that page base it scans 0x37 cells [code] — the 55 alien slots of the standard 5×11 formation — counting the non-zero (still-alive) ones into `ALIEN_COUNT` (0x2082) [seen] and raising `LAST_ALIEN_FLAG` (0x206b) [code] when exactly one survivor remains. Because the base comes from 0x2067, the same scan automatically reads whichever player's formation is currently active. Two variants reach into fixed offsets near the *top* of that page. `readActivePlayerPageTopByte` at 0x092e [code] builds the base and then forces the low byte to 0xff, reading the single status byte at the page's very top (`(mem[0x2067]<<8)|0xff`) back into A; its consumers (the object-timer paths around 0x1a7f and 0x0935) treat a zero there as "nothing pending" and bail. `activeFieldRecordPointer` at 0x0886 [code] instead forces the low byte to 0xfc, producing `HL = (mem[0x2067]<<8)|0xfc` — a pointer to a small per-player *field record* living just under the top of the page.

**The per-player field record.** That record holds the state that has to persist across a player's turns: a 16-bit reference-alien coordinate word at page offset 0xfc–0xfd, and a formation-count byte just below it at 0xfb. When the machine restores a player it loads this record — `loadReferenceAlienState` (0x00b1) [code] takes the 0x0886 pointer, reads the coordinate word out of the record, mirrors it into the working cells loc_2009 [guess] and `ALIEN_DRAW_ADDR` (0x200b) [code], then reads the count byte sitting one below the pointer, drops it by one when it reads exactly 0x03, stores it to loc_2008 [guess], and sets `FLEET_MOVE_DIR` (0x200d) [code] when that byte is the 0xfe edge sentinel. The save side is `stageActivePlayerFieldSave` at 0x0878 [code], the mirror image: it pulls the current count out of loc_2008 [guess] into B and the current coordinate word out of loc_2009 [guess] into DE, then builds the same 0x0886 record pointer in HL. It does not itself write the record — it *stages* the values and the destination, and its caller (the round-swap path at 0x02f8) performs the stores, dropping E and D into offsets 0xfc/0xfd and B back down at 0xfb. So 0878 and 00b1 are the outbound and inbound halves of swapping the active player's alien field in and out of that player's page.

**Selecting by the player bit.** Two routines ignore the page entirely and consult only the low bit of 0x2067. `currentPlayerRecordPtr` at 0x09ca [code] returns `HL = PLAYER1_OBJ_DESC` (0x20f8) [code] when that bit is set and `PLAYER2_OBJ_DESC` (0x20fc) [code] when it is clear — the two four-byte per-player object descriptors, four bytes apart. These carry per-player playfield state that lives outside the alien page; a consumer such as the alien-shot-rate selector (0x170e) takes this pointer and reads its offset+1 as the field-size key that picks the current fire rate, so the descriptor tracks how far along each player's field has been whittled down. `readActivePlayerInput` at 0x17c0 [code] uses the same bit to route hardware input: bit0 set reads IN 1 (player-one controls), bit0 clear reads IN 2 (player-two controls), returning the raw port byte in A. This is how the identical gameplay code drives whichever player's joystick and fire button is live.

**The record-totals accumulator.** `advanceRecordTotals` at 0x01d9 [seen] is the one primitive here confirmed against the hardware, and it is a small general-purpose accumulator rather than something tied to the player pages. Given HL pointing at (just before) a four-byte record, it treats offset +1 as the record's own stored delta, offset +2 and offset +3 as two running totals: it adds the caller's C into the first total (`[HL+2] += C`), adds the stored delta into the second (`[HL+3] += [HL+1]`, kept to eight bits), and returns that second total in A [seen]. It is a two-lane "step both accumulators by one tick" call, and the game reuses it for moving/animated objects — the animation record around 0x2006 (caller 0x01a1, role open [guess]) and the saucer record around 0x20c2 (caller 0x1868, role open [guess]) both drive their motion through it.

**Arming the round / attract demo.** `advanceRoundState` at 0x1618 [code] runs every frame as the pre-round gate that decides when the next player shot may be launched. It first insists the round is genuinely ready to advance: it bails unless loc_2015 [guess] reads 0xff, unless both `GAME_OBJECT_TABLE`'s head byte (0x2010) [code] and loc_2011 [guess] are zero (the field is idle, nothing in flight there), and unless `PLAYER_SHOT_STATUS` (0x2025) [code] is zero (no player shot already active). Only when all of that holds does it act, and it then splits on `GAME_IN_PROGRESS` (0x20ef) [code].

When 0x20ef is zero the machine is in attract mode and there is no real player, so firing is scripted: it sets `PLAYER_SHOT_STATUS` to 1 to launch a shot, then steps the 16-bit demo cursor `ATTRACT_DEMO_PTR` (0x20ed) [code] forward by one, wrapping its low byte back to 0x74 whenever it would reach 0x7e — cycling the cursor through a ten-byte window (0x74–0x7d) of a canned firing script — and publishes the byte it now points at to loc_201d [guess]. That way the attract-mode demo fires on a fixed pattern without touching the inputs.

When 0x20ef is non-zero a real game is on, so the launch is gated on the fire button (bit 0x10 of the player's input port, read through `readActivePlayerInput`, active-high) with a debounce held in `FIRE_BUTTON_LATCH` (0x202d) [code]. If the latch is already set, the routine is waiting for the previous press to end: it returns while the button is still held and clears the latch once the button reads released [code]. If the latch is clear, it is waiting for a fresh press: it returns while the button is up, and on a genuine new press it arms the shot — `PLAYER_SHOT_STATUS` (0x2025) := 1 and `FIRE_BUTTON_LATCH` (0x202d) := 1 [code] — so one press yields exactly one shot and holding the button down does not auto-repeat. This gate is invoked from the main frame loop (0x081f) and from the per-round setup/wait path (0x0aea) [code], which is why the same logic both starts the very first shot of a round and re-arms each subsequent one once the field falls quiet.

## Frame tasks, timers, boot, and scoring

**Boot and work-RAM setup.** When the machine resets, the vector at 0x0000 runs three no-ops and jumps to the boot routine at 0x18d4 [code]. That routine seats the stack pointer at 0x2400 — the top of work RAM — and clears the byte-count register to zero before calling the first initializer, `initWorkRam` [code] at 0x01e6. `initWorkRam` seeds a source of `WORKRAM_INIT_IMAGE` [code] (0x1b00, a fixed template held in ROM) and a destination of `ALIEN_DRAW_PENDING` [code] (0x2000, the base of work RAM), then copies bytes forward through the shared block-copy loop. Because the boot routine left the count register at zero and the copier treats a zero count as a full 256, this single call lays down a complete 0x100-byte page of initial state at the base of work RAM [code]. A companion copier, `loadDrawSequenceBlock` [code] at 0x0ae2, uses the same block-copy machinery at a smaller, fixed size: it moves a 12-byte (0x0c) block from a caller-supplied source into `loc_20c2` [guess], a fixed work-RAM slot whose exact role is still open.

**The game-active gate.** `GAME_ACTIVE` [seen] at 0x20e9 is a single on/off byte for all per-frame work. `setGameActive` [code] (0x19d1) writes 1 and `clearGameActive` [code] (0x19d7) writes 0, both funneling through the shared one-line store `loc_19d3` [seen], which simply deposits the accumulator into 0x20e9. Its consumers are the two raster interrupt handlers: the vblank handler at 0x0010 [code] and the mid-screen handler at 0x008c [code] each read `GAME_ACTIVE` early and, if it is zero, branch straight to the shared frame epilogue at 0x0082 without doing any per-frame processing. This one flag therefore switches the entire frame pipeline on and off.

**Per-frame task flags.** `TASK_FLAGS` [code] at 0x20c1 carries the frame's scheduling bits and is written by a dedicated one-line store, `loc_1982` [seen]. The mid-screen handler reads it, rotates its low bit into carry, and runs the draw pair (the object walk at 0x024b followed by a companion draw) only when that bit is set — but this gate applies only during attract: when `GAME_IN_PROGRESS` [code] (0x20ef) is nonzero the handler always draws and skips the `TASK_FLAGS` test entirely [code]. Elsewhere the same byte acts as a small phase selector — the sequencer at 0x0563 compares `TASK_FLAGS` against 0x04 to branch between phases [code].

**Timers and the object table.** The saucer countdown lives in `SAUCER_TIMER` [code] (0x2091), a 16-bit counter ticked once per frame by the vblank chain (0x0072 → 0x0913) [code]. The tick routine at 0x0913 first gates on `loc_2009` [guess], doing nothing while that byte is 0x78 or above; otherwise it decrements the 16-bit counter and stores it back. On the frame where the counter reaches zero it first reloads it from `TIMER_RELOAD` [code] (0x0600) and sets the wrap flag `loc_2083` [guess] to 1, so the counter rolls from 0x0600 back down and raises `loc_2083` once per cycle [code]. Running alongside the countdown is the per-frame object walk over `GAME_OBJECT_TABLE` [code] (0x2010): the vblank chain seats a pointer at 0x2010 (at 0x0248) and steps through a series of 16-byte records — a 0xff record ends the walk, a 0xfe record is skipped, and any live record has a 16-bit field tested and is either edited in place or used to dispatch a per-record handler [code]. The base byte of this table doubles as an idle signal: `advanceRoundState` [code] refuses to advance the round unless the byte at 0x2010 (and a neighboring cell) reads zero, i.e. the field is quiet [code].

**Scoring.** The score-add path is a deferred, one-shot request built from three cells. Two producers arm it. When a target is hit during play, the routine at 0x0a5f checks `GAME_IN_PROGRESS` [code] and, if a game is running, sounds a cue, looks a point value up from a small table, writes that byte into `SCORE_ADD_VALUE` [code] (0x20f2), zeroes the high byte `SCORE_ADD_VALUE_HI` [code] (0x20f3), and sets the pending flag `SCORE_ADD_PENDING` [code] (0x20f1) to 1 [code]. A second producer at 0x070c likewise sets 0x20f1 and stores a 16-bit value spanning 0x20f2/0x20f3 [code]. The consumer at 0x0988 runs later in the frame: if `SCORE_ADD_PENDING` is clear it returns immediately; otherwise it clears the flag and adds the two-byte little-endian delta at `SCORE_ADD_VALUE` into the current player's score accumulator digit-by-digit with decimal-adjust, propagating the BCD carry from the low byte up into the high byte [code]. In short, `SCORE_ADD_VALUE`/`SCORE_ADD_VALUE_HI` hold the pending delta while `SCORE_ADD_PENDING` is the once-only trigger that keeps a single scoring event from being credited twice.

**Cabinet setting, prize teardown, and a scaling helper.** `readStartingShips` [code] (0x08d1) reads player-input port 2, keeps its low two bits, and adds 3, yielding a 3-to-6 selector — the starting-ships count taken from the cabinet's setting [code]. `deactivatePrize` [code] (0x154a) tears down the bonus/prize state: it clears `PRIZE_ACTIVE` [code] (0x2002) and then, through `loc_19dc` [seen], ANDs the sound-port shadow `SOUND_PORT3_SHADOW` [seen] (0x2094) with 0xf7 to knock bit 3 off, writing the masked value both back to the shadow and out to sound port 3 so the associated sound stops; the masked byte is handed back in the accumulator [code]. Finally, `countStepsToThreshold` [code] (0x1554) is a small scaling helper: given a value in A and a threshold in H, it counts in C how many 0x10-sized steps lift A to or past H, first normalizing a negative A upward via `loc_1590` [code] (which keeps adding 0x10 and counting until the value is no longer negative). It returns the resulting step count with the carry deliberately cleared, a live result its callers read [code].

## What is not described yet

The control-flow spine — the main frame loop, the object-state dispatcher and its per-state handlers, the two
RST interrupt handlers, collision, and the attract-mode demo — is not yet recovered and is not described here.

- **`loc_0a5f`** stamps a points value (looked up through the three-entry table `loc_1da0` by a key its
  caller supplies) into the score-add cells, but whether its trigger is a hit reward or a bonus award is still open.
- **`loc_172c`** gates a sound bit on the player-shot status, but its exact role is unconfirmed.
- The reference-alien anchor pair **`loc_2009`/`loc_200a`** is known to be the rack-origin coordinate, but
  which byte is X and which is Y is not yet pinned. The saucer's timer sets a flag at **`loc_2083`** (very
  likely the saucer-active flag) and its sprite pointer sits at **`loc_2087`**; the alien-shot rate resolves
  into **`loc_20cf`** (read by the shot stepper `loc_0563`). The shared 11-byte object work buffer at
  **`loc_2073`** and its companion byte **`loc_207f`**, a 12-byte draw/animation block at **`loc_20c2`**, the
  round handshake flag **`loc_2015`**, the demo movement byte **`loc_201d`**, the fleet-active enable
